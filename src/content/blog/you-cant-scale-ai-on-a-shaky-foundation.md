You can't build a skyscraper on sand. And you can't scale AI on a shaky foundation.

Most AI projects don't fail because the technology is slow. They fail because the foundation underneath is unstable. A skyscraper on sand doesn't collapse because of the architecture. It collapses because of what's beneath it.

Any serious AI roadmap has two halves:

- the **foundational prerequisites**
- the **phased deployment timeline**

Most organisations jump straight to the second. The foundation is four layers. Skip one, and it resurfaces later as risk.

## 1. Data hygiene

Classification, labelling, deduplication. Miss these and the first thing your AI surfaces is sensitive data nobody meant to expose.

## 2. Security architecture

Access controls, identity governance, data loss prevention (DLP). This is the layer that fixes permission sprawl and enforces least privilege. Agents inherit whatever mess your permissions model already has.

## 3. Governance framework

AI policies, clear ownership, review cadences. Without named owners, accountability quietly disappears.

## 4. Licensing and financial controls

Entitlements, budgets, ROI gates. Spend discipline is a design decision, not an afterthought.

## Only then does the timeline make sense

```
Readiness
  → Pilot
  → Controlled rollout
  → Full-scale deployment
```

**Each phase earns the next by evidence, not by calendar date.**

## The uncomfortable part

Readiness work is invisible. Nobody applauds a classification project.

But it's the difference between a pilot that scales and one that quietly dies after the demo.

Worth asking of any organisation you work with: which of these four layers is weakest?
