I spend my days making sure AI features survive contact with real users — which
is a different job from making them survive contact with a demo audience. Most
of my career has been the seam between engineering and product: I can read a
model eval, I can also tell you why the eval doesn't match what a support
agent is about to experience at 2am on a Tuesday.

I started as a backend engineer, drifted into program management because I
liked deciding what to build more than how to build it, and landed on AI
product work because it's the rare corner of the industry where "we don't
actually know if this will work" is still a completely normal thing to say
out loud in a planning meeting.
